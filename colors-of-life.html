Chapter II · Palettes

Colors of Life

Four palettes, four times of day. Each is a small catalogue of works that share the same light and colour.

Terracotta

Afternoon · ochre & rust

The Yellow House · Van Gogh, 1888

Warm walls in the afternoon sun: ochre, rust, copper and the sepia of an old print. Terracotta is the colour of places that have been lived in for a long time — sun-faded plaster, clay pots, polished copper.

Sage

Morning · leaf & still water

The Japanese Footbridge · Monet, 1899

Soft greens of the morning: leaves, still water and a family breakfast in the shade. Sage is a quiet colour — it never shouts, it surrounds. These works feel cool, slow and full of air.

#7D8159#A9AD8A#DFE0CC#3F4430

Works in this palette

Dusk

Evening · blue hour

Old Battersea Bridge · Whistler, c. 1872

Blue hour and quiet rooms — the moment when the sky is not quite gone and the lamps are not yet lit. Dusk turns everything a little softer, a little further away, and invites a kind of thoughtful silence.

#6F7F8F#A6B3BD#E3E6E4#2F3A45

Works in this palette

Rosé

Golden hour · blossom & blush

Pink Peach Trees · Van Gogh, 1888

Blossom, ballet shoes and roses on a table — the softest pinks painters ever found in everyday life. Rosé is the colour of tenderness: a cradle, a spring orchard, a dancer waiting in the wings.
